# Time and date desktop widget

The **daylight window** and **Codex buddy** sit at the bottom-left of the desktop. The robot now has smooth, shaded vector artwork, a reflective faceplate, blinking eyes, rosy cheeks, soft floating movement, and a compact action bar. It stays sharp at every supported scale. Both widgets appear in **Customize widgets > Widgets and layout**, support Ctrl+drag, scaling, and hiding, and stay visible while the desktop is active by default.

**Ten little ways to play:** click the robot or **Pat** for a head pat, click **Treat** for a star cookie, or open **More +** / right-click for everything:

1. **Head pats:** happy eyes and floating hearts.
2. **Snack time:** star cookies, moon berries, and warm cloud tea, each with its own reaction.
3. **Tiny disco:** a silent dance with bouncing, swaying, and music notes.
4. **Dreamy naps:** two minutes of electric-sheep dreams; tap the buddy or choose Nap / wake up to wake early.
5. **Bubble pop:** a 20-second clicking game with a saved personal best.
6. **Sofa galaxy adventures:** 12-second expeditions that collect six different souvenirs. View them in the **Treasure shelf**.
7. **Wardrobe:** a sprout, crown, space cap, or the original bare antenna.
8. **Color palettes:** Blue, Mint, Peach, and Lilac. Your reminder buddy wears the same colors and hat.
9. **Tiny fortunes:** a rotating collection of whimsical local messages.
10. **Friendship levels:** earn stars through care and play, from New Friends to Cosmic Besties. Twenty stars per level, up to level 10; no streaks, decay, or penalties.

Starting another activity cancels the previous one; **Finish playing** returns to idle. Automatic reminders wait until the activity or nap finishes. Completed adventures give new treasures until the shelf is full. Colors, hats, friendship, treasure, and game records save in `companion-state.json`. Turning off smooth animation keeps activities usable with still poses. All play is silent, local, and requires no downloads. `Test-PetPlayground.ps1` exercises the activities, menus, persistence, cancellation, scaling, and reduced-motion behavior, and renders `preview-pet-playground.png` at 2x resolution.

The pet offers a silent check-in every randomly chosen **60-120 minutes**: the current local time, today's estimated active screen time, and a small suggestion such as getting water or relaxing your shoulders. A small bubble appears above applications for 22 seconds without taking keyboard focus. Reminders wait while Windows reports a busy/full-screen presentation state, the session is locked, or you are idle. Missed reminders do not accumulate. Choose **Tell me the time + a little nudge** in the right-click menu for a check-in any time. Right-click to **quiet it for 2 hours** or **mute automatic reminders**; the bubble also has snooze and dismiss buttons. Disabling the pet in settings disables its automatic reminders.

Screen time counts recent keyboard/mouse activity only while the widget process runs. It pauses after 60 seconds without input and excludes lock and long sleep/resume gaps; watching video or reading without input can be undercounted. It resets at local midnight and saves locally every minute and on normal exit in `companion-state.json`, together with mute/snooze preferences. This is an estimate, not Windows' complete historical usage. No app names, screenshots, keystrokes, or browsing history are recorded. Suggestions are a local rotating list, not messages generated by an online AI.

The daylight scene follows **Bangkok** sunrise/sunset from the same [Open-Meteo forecast](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) as the weather card. It shifts between dawn, daylight, golden hour, and a starry night, with the next sunrise/sunset countdown. Without current sunrise data it uses a clearly labeled approximate 06:00-18:00 scene. `Test-Companions.ps1` checks screen-time accounting, reminder suppression, persistence, daylight boundaries, placement and controls, and renders `preview-companions.png`.

A transparent white clock centered on the primary screen, using only Windows PowerShell and Windows' built-in WPF components. Nothing is downloaded or installed.

Double-click **Start Widget.vbs** to start. Double-click **Stop Widget.cmd** to close it. Starting twice does not create a second widget. You can also right-click the widget to close or re-center it.

Double-click **Customize Widgets.vbs**, or right-click any widget and choose **Customize widgets...**, to open the settings panel. It includes:

- **Appearance:** Glass, Midnight, or Warm cards; five accent colors; glass opacity; smooth animation toggle; and the delay before a card hides. Glass is the default. Calendar highlights, the battery ring, weather icon, and system bars share the accent.
- **Widgets and layout:** show/hide each card, pin it while the desktop is active, resize it from 70% to 150%, and edit its position offsets. Hold **Ctrl and drag** any card or the clock to move it. Hidden cards pass clicks through. Positions stay inside the primary screen's work area; size changes preserve the default gaps between photo/weather and calendar/battery. **Reset positions and sizes** prepares the original layout; click Apply to save it.
- **Clock and details:** six clock styles, four clock text colors, 65–150% clock size, 12/24-hour time, seconds, date display, media visibility, photo Fill/Fit framing, note text size, and Celsius/km/h or Fahrenheit/mph for Bangkok weather. Photo selection saves immediately; the other settings save with **Apply changes**.

The panel uses the same visual language as the cards, with a theme sample and keyboard-accessible controls. Changes apply without restarting. Shared appearance and layout live in `widget-settings.json`, saved atomically; existing clock preferences, dock shortcuts, photos, and notes are preserved. Closing the settings panel discards unapplied changes. The clock stays available as an entry point even if all cards are disabled.

`Test-Customization.ps1` checks settings validation, nested persistence, UI application, clock formatting, weather conversions, scaling, screen bounds, drag completion, and layout reset, and renders the three settings tabs for review.

The largest line is the English weekday. Below it are the 24-hour time (`HH:mm`) and a smaller date (`d/M/yyyy`, for example `26/9/2026`).

Right-click anywhere in the clock area and choose **Style**:

- **Smooth Mond** (default): thin geometric lettering with curved shapes and rounded ends.
- **Original Mond**: angular geometric lettering.
- **Minimal**: light Segoe UI lettering.
- **Classic**: italic Georgia lettering.
- **Bold**: strong Bahnschrift capitals.
- **Digital**: monospaced Consolas capitals.

Choose **Text color** for White, Warm ivory, Ice blue, or Soft pink. Changes appear immediately and are saved in `settings.json` for next time. All lettering is drawn locally or uses installed Windows fonts. Nothing is downloaded. Geometric lettering is inspired by Mond, not an exact copy of its font. Grayscale antialiasing and rounded vector strokes keep the transparent text smooth at the screen's native scale.

The clock reads your computer's local time every second, including after sleep and date or time-zone changes. Set Windows' clock correctly for accurate time. It uses a transparent top-level window owned by the desktop, behind normal applications. Screen placement uses WPF coordinates to respect Windows display scaling. The clock's visibility and background position are refreshed every second.

Under the date is a media timeline connected to Windows' current media session. Start a video in your browser or a compatible media player, then return to the desktop:

- Click or drag the timeline to seek.
- Click **-10 s** or **+10 s** to move backward or forward ten seconds.
- Click **Play / Pause** to control playback.
- The video title, elapsed time, and total duration update automatically. The timeline follows playback speed and remains still when paused.

This uses the built-in Windows media API; no extension, installation, or download is needed. The player must expose media controls to Windows. If no video is available, the widget says **Play a video to connect**. Seeking is disabled when the player does not expose a seekable timeline, such as some live streams. With several players, controls target the current Windows media session. A rejected command is reported instead of pretending it succeeded.

`Test-Media.ps1` checks formatting, clamping, playback-rate interpolation, and paused positions. `Test-MediaControls.ps1` checks the real current session without changing playback. Its optional `-VerifySeek` flag tests forward/back and timeline handlers on paused media, then restores the original position.

Run `Enable-WidgetStartup.ps1` to create **Desktop Widgets** shortcuts on your desktop and in your Windows startup folder. The widget will start silently when you sign in to Windows. Double-click the desktop shortcut to start it manually; starting it again does not create duplicates. Closing and reopening the laptop lid normally keeps the running widget through sleep. Keep this project folder in place, or rerun the setup script after moving it. To disable automatic startup, press Win+R, enter `shell:startup`, and delete **Desktop Widgets** from that folder.

Calendar and battery cards also live in the **top-left of the desktop**, as two separate compact windows with a translucent liquid-glass-style finish and reflective rims. The calendar is on the left and the battery is beside it with a gap. Move the pointer near an individual card to reveal just that card. By default, each fades in from 20 pixels above over 0.22 seconds, stays visible while you use it, and fades upward after you move away for 0.6 seconds. Customize the hide delay or turn off animation in settings. At 100% size the calendar window is 214 by 216 display-scaled pixels; the battery window is 186 by 216. Hidden cards let desktop clicks pass through. They stay behind applications and do not open over browser tabs when you move to a top-left tab.

The calendar starts on Monday, highlights today, and follows the real date. Use the arrows to browse months; click the month heading to return to the current month. The battery ring shows the real Windows battery percentage and whether it is charging or plugged in, refreshing every 10 seconds. PCs without a battery show **No battery detected**. These two cards start and stop with the main widget. The center clock and media controls keep their position.

Run `ClockWidget.ps1 -PreviewCorner` in Windows PowerShell for a corner-card preview. `Test-CornerWidgets.ps1` verifies calendar alignment, leap years, six-row months, hover boundaries, and hidden click-through behavior.

A matching **Bangkok weather card** lives in the **top-right**. Hover near that corner on the desktop to reveal it independently; it fades down from above and hides when you move away, just like the other cards. It shows temperature in Celsius, conditions, feels-like temperature, humidity, wind in km/h, and the weather report's Bangkok timestamp.

A **photo widget** sits immediately to the left of the weather card with a 14-pixel gap. Both windows measure 214 by 244 display-scaled pixels. Hover to reveal the matching rounded frame and click it to choose a local photo. Pictures fill the frame with a centered crop. Right-click to change or remove the photo. The file path is saved in `photo-settings.json`; the original image stays in its location and is not locked or modified. If it moves or becomes unavailable, click the placeholder to choose again. Preview mode does not load your personal photo.

Weather comes from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/) and refreshes every 10 minutes using an asynchronous request, so the clock and animations remain responsive. Internet access is needed, but no installation or API key is required. On failure, the card marks retained readings as offline and retries after a minute; if it has no readings, it shows weather unavailable. Only Bangkok's fixed city coordinates are requested; device location is not accessed. `Test-Weather.ps1 -Live` verifies the API connection and creates a preview using live readings.

Use **Customize widgets...** to change sizes and colors. For source-level typography changes, `StrokeThickness` in `ClockWidget.ps1` controls geometric weekday weight.

The original **left app dock** stays halfway down the left edge with five shortcuts, independent hover reveal, drag-to-reorder, and its existing `dock-settings.json` preferences.

A separate **macOS taskbar** sits at the bottom above the Windows taskbar. It spans 96% of the screen by default and shows Windows Start, File Explorer, independently customizable pinned apps and running apps, spaced evenly across the bar, with neighboring-icon magnification and running indicators. Overflow can be scrolled horizontally with the mouse wheel. Right-click for pinned-app configuration, an Open windows submenu, and widget Settings. Clicking a pinned running app restores/switches to it; repeated clicks cycle its windows. All open windows remain accessible through the right-click Open windows submenu. First-run pins copy the left dock; subsequent changes are stored separately in `mac-dock-settings.json`. Shortcut matching uses resolved executable paths; unmatched apps remain available as running tasks.

The **top menu bar** has real Windows folder/system menus, power status, and a live clock. While using an app, the taskbar hides until the pointer stays at the physical bottom edge for one second. Leaving the edge resets the timer. Once revealed, it stays open while hovered or while its menu is open, then uses the normal hide delay. On the desktop it retains its existing pin/hover behavior. By default the top bar hides until the pointer stays within four pixels of the top edge for one continuous second; leaving resets the timer. After reveal, the normal hide delay applies, and menus keep it open. Both bars hide for full-screen windows. PIN can keep the top bar permanently visible. They are Windows-compatible recreations, not the macOS shell or application menus. The Windows taskbar remains available.

Settings uses sidebar navigation, rounded panels, toggle switches and a matching rounded right-click menu throughout the widget suite. A **Dock width** slider controls 50–100% of screen width independently of icon size. All three have separate **SHOW, PIN and SIZE** controls under **Customize widgets > Widgets and layout**. PIN keeps the taskbar/top bar visible over apps; unpin enables hover reveal. The left dock retains its desktop-only behavior. The top bar stays aligned to the screen, so its position fields are disabled. `Test-DesktopBars.ps1` checks rendering and settings; `Test-MacTaskbar.ps1` uses a disposable real window to check task detection, running dots, minimized restoration, overlays and independent disable.

A matching **Quick Note** card sits directly below the weather widget. Hover over that area to reveal it, then click inside to type. It stays visible while the editor has keyboard focus, even if you move the pointer away. Click **Done**, press **Esc**, or switch to another app to finish editing. Notes save locally to `quick-note.txt` after a short typing pause, when the card loses focus, and when the widget closes. The editor supports multiple lines and scrolling. The status line reports unsaved changes or a save failure. No app installation, certificate, notification permission, or Developer Mode is used. Preview mode does not read or write your private note.

The notification prototype is no longer loaded. The ordinary launcher goes directly to `ClockWidget.ps1`; unused notification installer/build files are not part of startup. `Test-DockAndNote.ps1` checks shortcut reordering and persistence, drag-click suppression, Unicode note saves and edits, empty notes, failed-save retention, and removal of the notification placeholder. `Test-CornerWidgets.ps1` also checks quick-note placement, keyboard activation, and independent reveal/click-through behavior.

For a local rendering check, run Windows PowerShell with `-NoProfile -STA -File .\ClockWidget.ps1 -Preview`. This creates `preview.png` at 2x resolution and exits. Use `-PreviewAll` to exercise every style's menu handler and generate separate previews without changing saved preferences. `status.json` records launch diagnostics.

A compact **system monitor** lives at the **top center**, with CPU, RAM, and system-drive storage usage. Hover near it on the desktop to reveal it. CPU and RAM refresh every two seconds; disk usage refreshes every 30 seconds. Hover a metric for its details, including RAM capacity and free storage. DISK means storage space used, not disk activity. The first CPU reading appears after a sampling interval; unavailable readings show `--`. The monitor uses built-in Windows APIs and needs no installation or administrator access.

The **quick-folder dock** sits on the **right side**, below the weather/note cards to avoid overlap. On shorter screens it moves beside those cards while staying on the right. Hover to reveal its four folder shortcuts: Downloads, Documents, Pictures, and this widget's project folder. Downloads and the other standard folders follow Windows' configured locations. Hover an icon for its folder name; click to open it in Explorer. Right-click and choose **Change folder 1...** through **Change folder 4...** to customize them. Choices are saved locally in `folder-settings.json`. It shares the app dock's icon hover animation and slides in from the right. Hidden cards pass clicks through and remain behind normal applications.

`Test-SystemAndFolders.ps1` checks CPU sampling math and live CPU/RAM/storage readings, top-center placement, folder-dock collision avoidance on normal and shorter screens, saved folder choices, rejection of file paths, and hidden click-through behavior.

To append a pinned app, right-click the bottom dock and choose **Add another app...**, then select a .lnk, .url, or .exe file. Existing pins stay in place, duplicate paths are ignored, and the new pin saves automatically. Drag it to reorder. **Pinned apps** still replaces existing slots. Test-AddPinnedApp.ps1 verifies appending, saving, dynamic button/menu creation, validation and reordering.


The bottom dock now uses a visible underline for running apps (blue and wider for the active app), plus a count badge for multiple windows. Hover a grouped app for 0.4 seconds, or click it, to open a selectable window list. Pinned and unpinned windows group by executable path. The picker shows actual window titles, including Chrome profile names when Chrome supplies them in the title; it does not read browser profile data. Move away to dismiss it.

The **^ System tray** button reveals and focuses the original Windows notification area. Use its hidden-icons arrow and app menus normally. If the Windows taskbar is hidden in widget settings, it returns to hidden after you leave the tray. This uses the actual Windows tray, not a list of all background processes. Startup remains controlled by the existing **Desktop Widgets** shortcut in `shell:startup`, which Windows may display as `wscript.exe`.


The top menu bar now includes macOS-inspired rounded Wi-Fi, Sound, Bluetooth, Battery, Calendar, Search and Control Center panels. Wi-Fi lists nearby networks using Windows WLAN APIs, marks a connected network, and connects saved profiles; new networks open Windows Wi-Fi Settings for password setup. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth switches use Windows radio permissions and report denials. Sound controls the default Windows audio endpoint through a live volume slider and mute button. Output-device selection, Bluetooth pairing, Focus, projection and display adjustments open the appropriate Windows settings. Apple-only services and application-specific Mac menus are not emulated. Click outside or press Escape to dismiss a panel. Test-TopBarPanels.ps1 renders every main panel and checks the audio read/write roundtrip without changing the current volume.


The menu bar uses a Windows logo. Dropdowns have a compact glass design with gradient highlights, subtle hover/focus borders, and a continuous pill volume fill; mute sits beside the sound label. Layered WPF popups use a rounded opaque gradient; native acrylic is disabled on those windows because it paints square corners outside the alpha mask. This is a Windows glass treatment, not Apple's Liquid Glass renderer. Rendering tests also check volume-fill geometry at 0%, 50%, and 100% without changing the system volume.
